CrowdStrike plans $43.9 billion acquisition of Zscaler

The companies say the proposed combination would bring endpoint, workload and network security closer together. The deal still needs shareholder and regulatory approvals.

Raazim Tech Editorial DeskPublished 24 November 2026 · 10:54 EAT3 min read

Cybersecurity companies CrowdStrike and Zscaler have announced a definitive agreement under which CrowdStrike will acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction valued at approximately $43.9 billion. The companies announced the agreement on November 23, 2026.

Under the announced terms, Zscaler shareholders would receive 1.135 shares of CrowdStrike common stock for each Zscaler share. The companies said the transaction value was calculated using CrowdStrike's closing share price on November 20.

What the companies would bring together

CrowdStrike is known for endpoint and workload protection through its Falcon platform. Zscaler provides cloud-based network security and Zero Trust access controls. The proposed combination would bring these areas together under a broader security platform.

The companies argue that organizations increasingly need to secure not only employees and devices, but also cloud applications, workloads and AI agents that access business systems. Their stated plan is to combine endpoint protection with network-level Zero Trust controls.

The deal is not completed

The agreement has been approved by both companies' boards, but it still requires Zscaler shareholder approval, regulatory approvals and other customary closing conditions. The companies expect closing in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal year 2028. Until the transaction closes, both businesses will continue to operate independently.

CrowdStrike says the combined company is expected to begin with more than $10 billion in annual recurring revenue. That figure is a company expectation for the combined business, not a completed post-merger result.

Why the deal matters

The proposed acquisition reflects a wider shift in enterprise security. As organizations use more cloud services and automated software agents, security controls need to cover identities, devices, applications and the connections between them. Combining endpoint and network security could make it easier for customers to manage these controls through a broader platform.

However, a large acquisition does not automatically produce a seamless product. Customers will want clarity on product roadmaps, integrations, pricing, support and how existing contracts will be handled. Regulators and shareholders also need to review the proposed transaction.

Raazim Tech takeaway

The headline figure is large, but the practical test will be execution: whether the companies can integrate their products without adding complexity for the organizations that depend on them.

What to watch next

Key milestones include the shareholder vote, regulatory review, integration plans and any changes to the expected closing timetable. Until closing, customers should treat CrowdStrike and Zscaler as separate operating companies.
