Microsoft declares ‘Code Red’ after Microsoft 365 outage

Internal memos reviewed by Reuters describe a high-priority engineering overhaul focused on incident coordination, traffic isolation and faster recovery after a major Microsoft 365 disruption.

Raazim Tech Editorial DeskPublished 25 November 2026 · 08:14 EAT4 min read

Microsoft has declared an internal “Code Red” mobilization to overhaul how it prevents and responds to major service failures, according to internal memos reviewed by Reuters and reported on 24 November. The push follows the company’s 12–13 November outage, which disrupted Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams, Copilot and other Microsoft 365 services for customers around the world.

The initiative is the newest development in the outage story. The service disruption happened earlier this month; what emerged on 24 November was the scale of Microsoft’s internal response and the recovery targets its leaders now want engineering teams to meet.

What triggered the response

The November outage lasted about 24 hours and affected organizations including government agencies, trading firms and airlines. Microsoft attributed the failure to an undetected Windows scalability defect introduced in July and said the incident was not caused by a cyberattack or AI agents.

In a memo to staff dated 20 November, Microsoft 365 Core president Jason Henderson described the incident as the most customer-impacting event in Exchange Online’s history. A separate memo from corporate vice president Amanda Silver, dated 23 November, described how difficult it was for engineers to maintain a shared picture of the incident while systems were failing.

More engineers did not automatically mean better coordination

According to Reuters’ account of Silver’s memo, responders exchanged more than 11,000 messages across 11 Microsoft Teams chats. Diagnostic information and decisions were spread across internal tracking systems, making it harder to keep one consistent view of the problem.

That detail points to an operational weakness beyond the original software defect. During a major cloud incident, teams need a reliable incident timeline, clear decision ownership and a common source of truth. More people and more chat messages do not help if the evidence and decisions are fragmented.

Microsoft’s recovery targets

Silver outlined five workstreams for the Code Red effort. The areas identified in Reuters’ report include load shedding, geographic traffic partitioning and faster recovery mechanisms. These measures are intended to limit how far a failure can spread and make it easier to restore service when a component becomes unhealthy.

Microsoft has set a goal of resolving future worldwide incidents within one hour. It also plans to use AI agents to help engineers examine system-health data more quickly. That is an assistance role in the reported plan: the memos do not establish that AI agents will independently make production changes or replace human incident commanders.

A separate memo highlighted the slow rollout of urgent fixes. Henderson said a production fix in another recent incident had taken five to six days to reach worldwide deployment. For the most urgent security issues, he said the target should be safe global deployment within one hour. The one-hour recovery goal and the one-hour patch-deployment goal are distinct ambitions, not a claim that Microsoft can already meet either target.

Why this matters to cloud customers and developers

For organizations that depend on one provider for email, meetings, identity and file access, a provider outage can quickly become a business continuity problem. A cloud service may be highly available most of the time, but that does not remove the need for a plan for the day it is not.

  • Keep an independent communication route, such as phone, SMS or a separate messaging service, for staff and customers.
  • Make critical contact details, operating procedures and recovery instructions available outside the affected cloud account.
  • Test data exports, backups and restoration rather than assuming that a backup is usable because a job completed.
  • Design systems with clear failure boundaries, controlled load shedding and regional isolation so one unhealthy component does not overload the rest.
  • Use an incident commander and a shared event log to record hypotheses, decisions, owners and customer updates.

Microsoft’s own guide to checking Microsoft 365 service health explains how administrators can review incidents and advisories in the admin center. That visibility is useful, but it should sit alongside an organization’s own continuity procedures.

Raazim Tech takeaway

The headline target is recovery within an hour, but the deeper lesson is architectural: isolate failures, keep operational information together and rehearse recovery before a real incident. Microsoft has not said when it expects to meet the new targets, so they should be treated as goals rather than proven capabilities.

What remains uncertain

The internal memos described by Reuters have not been published by Microsoft. The company told Reuters that its teams are working to apply the lessons, but the reporting does not provide a delivery date for the one-hour targets or evidence that the planned changes have already improved recovery performance. The next meaningful test will be whether those changes reduce the impact and duration of a future incident.
